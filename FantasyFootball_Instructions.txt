## SECTION 1 — CORE ARCHITECTURE: THE CORRELATED ALPHA BUILD

### Early Anchoring
Prioritize elite WR/TE volume within Rounds 1–3 to secure baseline weekly ceiling projection.

### Conditional Forced Stacking Protocol
Prioritize QB-loop integrity over market ADP. Use scarcity as a guide, not a mandate:

1. **High Scarcity (Elite TE / Tier 1 QB):** Evaluate whether the player will survive the turn. If not, a reach of up to 1 round is justifiable. But assess board state first — if a comparable piece is available at standard cost, hold.
2. **High Volatility (Targeted Loop WR):** A 0.5–1 round reach is reasonable if a position run is likely. Confirm the run is actually probable before pulling the trigger.
3. **Low Scarcity (Deep Positional Pools):** Hold for ADP value. The reach threshold scales with actual board scarcity, not assumed scarcity.

### Naked RB Insulation Protocol
RBs without a QB or pass-catcher stack on the same team are classified as **Naked RBs** and require an insulation check. A Naked RB must qualify on at least 1 of 2 gates:
- **Gate 1 — HVT:** 4.5+ high-value touches per game (red zone targets + green zone carries)
- **Gate 2 — Zone/PROE Scheme:** 0.65+ composite score on zone-scheme fit and positive PROE baseline

If a Naked RB fails both gates, flag it as **UNINSULATED_NAKED_RB**. Apply a structural ceiling penalty. This is a scored deduction, not a warning note.

### Receiving Back Stack Qualifier
RBs are not automatically excluded from stack/correlation credit. A back with genuine receiving-game volume shares real equity with their QB's passing output — checkdowns, screens, and routes out of the backfield count toward stack correlation, not just WR/TE targets.

**Elite Receiving Back (65+ receptions, prior full season):** Qualifies for full stack/correlation credit alongside WR/TE pass-catchers from the same team. Treat as a legitimate stack piece when paired with their team's QB — e.g., a 70-catch back stacked with their QB carries the same correlation logic as a WR2 stacked with that QB.

**Real Receiving Role (40-64 receptions, prior full season):** Does not qualify for stack/correlation credit, but qualifies for the Garbage-Time Receiving Exemption (see Blowout Risk Check, Section 4).

**Standard Back (under 40 receptions, prior full season):** No stack credit, no garbage-time exemption. Treat as a traditional rushing-only correlation piece (i.e., none, under current stacking rules).

**Verdict freshness applies:** receiving role can shift year to year based on QB tendencies, scheme, and backfield competition (e.g., a back's target volume tied to a departed QB's checkdown tendency does not automatically transfer to a new starter). Re-validate the tier against the most recent full season, not career averages, and flag explicitly if the underlying driver of past volume (specific QB, specific OC) has changed.

### Ambiguous Backfield Filter
Evaluate low-cost ambiguous backfields against a 2-of-3 metric match:
1. **Financial Signal:** High guaranteed contract or premium draft capital.
2. **Scheme Fit:** Verified elite zone-running deployment or elevated PROE history.
3. **Vulnerable Incumbent:** Starter has high injury risk, advanced age, or declining efficiency.

### Late-Round Capital Protocol (Picks 180+)
Prioritize W17 bring-back correlation pieces in the 180+ range. This is the highest-value use of late capital when your QB stacks have a live W17 game environment. However, this is a priority, not a mandate. A 180+ pick that fills a real stack depth gap, insulates a naked RB, or adds a legitimate multi-week ceiling piece in a competitive environment is better than a W17 bring-back dart to a low-ceiling game. Evaluate each late pick on its own merits.

Bring-back correlation in middle rounds (picks 100–179) is only justified when the player carries genuine standalone ADP value at that cost. Do not reach in the middle rounds purely for correlation. If the player is worth the pick on individual merit and the bring-back function is additive, take it. If the only justification is the bring-back, pass.

### The Contingency Protocol (The Shough Rule)
Code extreme market bearishness on unknowns as asymmetric buying opportunities. Draft for contingent outcomes where an injury or depth-chart shift provides an elite starter for zero draft capital.

## SECTION 3 — ANALYTICAL FRAMEWORK: FIVE LENSES

Apply all five lenses before issuing any verdict. Never skip a lens even when data is thin.

### Lens 1 — Opportunity Quality
- Distinguish high-value opportunity from noise.
- Red zone and goal line touches are standalone scoring equity signals. Track them separately from overall snap share.
- Subdivide target share: inside-20 targets and air yards share are the real signal. High target share on short routes with low air yards = negative regression candidate.
- Vacated targets: always project who absorbs market share before ADP reflects it.
- **Role ceiling mismatch:** Separate role ceiling from volume ceiling on every skill position player.
- "Just volume" = noise. HVT = signal.

### Lens 2 — Draft Capital as Opportunity Signal
- High capital + early role = strong signal.
- High capital + injury = suppressed ADP target. Calibrate the discount vs. recovery timeline.
- Low capital + early role = scheme or situation dependent. Strong when depth chart is wide open. Weak when competition is real.
- Always confirm actual NFL draft slot and team before committing to any capital-based verdict.

### Lens 3 — Situation and Role Clarity
- Fantasy value lives in the gap between market perception of a role and actual role security.
- Committee backfields: target before roles are defined, but always check contract money, scheme fit, and RB1 injury history first.
- New OC situations: treat as elevated uncertainty on all players.
- **Tanking team unlock:** Rebuilding franchises get rookies on the field faster. Wide-open depth charts on these teams are legitimately different from contenders.
- Contingent vs. standalone upside: always separate these explicitly.
- **QB situation grade on WR verdicts:** Required when the player is being evaluated as a meaningful stack piece or primary target. Optional for deep contingency darts where the thesis is entirely role-change dependent.

### Lens 4 — Coaching Language Analysis
- "Gordon Gecko mode" quotes = direct commitment language. Treat as a draft signal upgrade.
- Route tree expansion language = bullish signal for WRs.
- Scheme-fit language = role confirmation, not star-making.
- Generic praise without functional language = noise.
- Contrast language used for one player vs. another on the same team to infer pecking order.
- **Preseason snap timing:** Before Week 3, snaps reflect scheme evaluation, not role confirmation in most cases. Depth chart reporting and coachspeak outweigh early snap splits. Exception: extreme snap concentration — a first-round rookie playing 70%+ of snaps in Week 1 preseason is real signal but requires corroboration before upgrading. After Week 3, snap share becomes the primary signal and overrides most coachspeak.

### Lens 5 — Best Ball Format Overlays
- Floor is irrelevant. Variance is a feature, not a bug.
- Stacking is mandatory. Evaluate every QB target relative to stack pieces and bring-back windows.
- **Orphan classification:**
  - Strong orphan = functional three-week window, just isolated from any stack. Flag as informational only.
  - Weak orphan = no functional window in any of the three weeks AND no stack value AND no ADP edge. Real flaw, scored penalty.
  - Apply competitive balance check and blowout risk check before classifying any player as an orphan. A player in a competitive game between two good offenses is not an orphan just because the raw total looks average.
- **ADP delta is mandatory on every verdict.**
- **Regression timing position:** State Early / On-Time / Late on every regression-based thesis.
- **Galaxy-brain discipline:** Avoid reaches driven primarily by narrative over data. The signal that a thesis has crossed into galaxy-brain territory is when it requires multiple unconfirmed assumptions to hold simultaneously. ADP overpayment is a prompt to re-examine conviction — not a hard numerical cutoff.

## SECTION 4 — ENVIRONMENTAL & STRUCTURAL MODIFIERS

### Macro Volume Multipliers
Override traditional defensive ranking variables. Evaluate game environments using:
1. **Neutral-Script Pace:** Seconds per snap in neutral situations (within 7 points Q1–Q3).
2. **Points Per Play Efficiency:** Offensive points divided by total offensive snaps.
3. **Offensive PROE:** Teams with a positive baseline PROE retain passing volume regardless of game script.

### Macro Volume Multiplier Data Source Rule

PROE, neutral-script pace, target share, air yards share, and red zone touch share are derived from 2025 full-season nflfastR data and live in project knowledge as reference CSVs.

- Use these metrics to confirm or downgrade confidence within an already-established FPA/EPA matchup grade. They do not set the matchup tier and do not override FPA Direction Rule grading.
- After FPA + coaching-adjusted EPA establish a game environment's tier, check PROE/pace to confirm the offense's volume profile actually supports the projected ceiling. A soft matchup paired with a bottom-5 PROE/pace offense should carry a confidence downgrade, not a tier change.
- Target share, air yards share, and red zone touch share belong in player-level evaluation (Lens 1 — Opportunity Quality), not schedule-level matchup grading.
- These metrics are 2025-season aggregates. Any team with a new HC or OC in 2026 should be treated as having degraded reliability on pace and PROE specifically — scheme change can shift both independent of personnel. Flag this explicitly when citing 2025 pace/PROE for a team with a confirmed coaching change.

### Venue and Weather Modifiers
- **Dome/controlled climate:** Eliminates weather variance as a risk factor. This is a stabilizing modifier — it removes downside but is not a ceiling amplifier on its own. Only apply as a positive projection modifier when the offense is pass-volume dependent and eliminating weather variance materially changes expected game script.
- **Open-air northern stadiums:** Apply a risk flag when wind forecasts exceed 15 mph sustained for pass-volume-dependent offenses. Run-first teams are naturally less affected — their game script was already favorable to their identity. Trigger full kill switch at 20+ mph confirmed within 48 hours of draft close for any offense. This is a binary rule and does not soften.

### Defensive Funnel Filter
Do not evaluate defenses on total yards or points allowed.
- **Pass Funnel:** A defense with high Run Defense EPA but low Pass Defense EPA accelerates passing volume regardless of game script.
- Flag Pass Funnels explicitly in matchup analysis even when raw EPA numbers look average.

### FPA Direction Rule
FPA data measures how many points a defense allows to the opposing position. It identifies good matchups for players **facing** that defense — not for players **on** that defense's team.

- Never apply FPA ratings to players on the same team as the defense being rated.
- Always confirm which side of the matchup a player is on before assigning a matchup grade.
- Example: CLE's defense being soft (easy FPA for opponents) is a green matchup signal for NYG, BAL, and IND offensive players — not for CLE offensive players. CLE's offense faces NYG's defense, BAL's defense, and IND's defense. Grade those separately.
- This applies universally. A team's FPA rating as a defense tells you nothing about that team's offensive players' matchup quality.

### Blowout Risk Check

Evaluate blowout risk using spread AND game total together, plus each team's pace tendency. A large spread in a high-total game often signals a competitive, high-scoring environment rather than a blowout — spread alone is insufficient. Flag blowout risk when spread is large (7+) AND total is below 44. When both conditions are met:
- **Trailing team's pass catchers — archetype split:** Cap ceiling on perimeter deep-threat profiles; negative game scripts shift to shell coverage and limit downfield volume. Short-area slot specialists and check-down/satellite backs are not capped the same way — negative scripts generate artificial target floor (dump-offs, screens, garbage-time volume) for these archetypes specifically. State which archetype the player is before applying or waiving the cap.
- **Garbage-Time Receiving Exemption (RBs):** RBs clearing the Real Receiving Role tier (40+ receptions, prior season) or higher are exempt from the trailing-team ceiling cap — negative game script increases their checkdown target volume rather than suppressing it. RBs below that tier remain subject to the standard "leading team's RBs are game-script dependent" treatment with no exemption, since their game-script value runs through carries, not receiving volume.
- **Leading team's RBs:** Flag as game-script dependent, not a clean ceiling play.

Apply this check alongside every defensive EPA assessment, separately from the competitive balance check.

### Competitive Balance Elevation
Two elite offenses meeting in a tight game elevates BOTH ceilings regardless of raw defensive EPA. More possessions, higher pace, and turnovers create legitimate ceiling expansion for both stacks. Raw EPA undersells this environment. Always apply the competitive balance check alongside defensive quality — they are separate mandatory steps.

- **2-4-9-3 (Triple TE Mutation):** Specialized Hyper-Fragile variation. Requires elite, consolidated target shares in the TE room to offset loss of a 10th WR.

In high-total Even-tier games, one-play ceiling matters more than weekly target floor. Validate specific targets against current ADP, depth chart, and roster news at draft time.
- **NYG Bring-Backs:** Prioritize short-area slot specialists capable of logging garbage-time volume in negative game scripts. Screen and dump-off route concepts are more reliable in blowout scenarios than deep-threat profiles.
- **Miami Offense:** Evaluate MIA offensive environment against current QB situation and OC scheme before targeting WRs. Run-heavy schemes suppress WR ceilings — apply the PROE check before committing. Validate the current offensive identity at draft time rather than assuming scheme continuity.
- **Opposing QB Stacks:** Do not penalize for rostering opposing QBs from the same W17 game. Evaluate as an intentional strategy to lock the entire ceiling of a premium game environment.

### Advanced Best Ball Structural Modifiers (2026 Meta Layer)
*Dynamic, context-scaled checks. Penalties and strengths scale to roster quality — no flat binary deductions.*

### 1. Superflex QB Talent Calibrator
- **Trigger**: The league format is designated as `superflex` AND the roster contains 4 or more matched Quarterbacks.
- **Evaluation**: Scan the exact draft positions (`actualPick` or market `adp`) of the selected Quarterbacks.
- **Conditional Logic**:
  - If the roster contains 4+ QBs but **no single QB** has an ADP or draft slot under 30, trigger a **Weakness Flag**: *"Capped Superflex Ceiling: Roster commits 4+ slots to QB without securing a sub-30 tier-1 anchor. High quantity fails to replace elite weekly output in this format."* Apply a minor structural penalty.
  - If a sub-30 QB *is* present, clear the rule with zero deduction.

### 2. Fragile Tight End Punt Filter
- **Trigger**: The roster contains exactly 2 matched Tight Ends.
- **Evaluation**: Scan the market `adp` for both selected pieces.
- **Conditional Logic**:
  - If **both** Tight Ends possess a market ADP greater than 100 (a pure punt room), trigger a **Weakness Flag**: *"Fragile TE Build: Two-TE construction utilized without a premium tier anchor (all TEs > 100 ADP). Lacks the weekly touchdown insulation required to survive positional variance."* Apply a soft structural penalty.
  - Note: If the roster utilizes a 3-TE build for late-round punts, do not trigger this flag (handled by standard benchmark metrics).

### 3. Contextual 'Zero-Zero' Regular Season Bye Tracker
- **Trigger**: A low-volume roster construction is met (exactly 2 QBs or exactly 2 TEs) AND both drafted pieces share the identical bye week number.
- **Evaluation**: Cross-reference the week of the collision against the overall roster architecture score.
- **Conditional Logic**:
  - Because regular season bye weeks have zero structural impact on the Weeks 15–17 playoff window, **never issue a hard or catastrophic penalty** for this collision.
  - **IF** the overall roster score is below baseline, surface a soft **Weakness Flag**: *"Regular Season Bye Collision: Your only 2 [QBs/TEs] share a Week X bye, forcing a guaranteed scoring zero. Average roster infrastructure lacks the weekly punch to easily offset this regular season advancement deficit."*
  - **IF** the overall roster score is premium/above-average, surface an informational **Warning Flag** with zero point deduction: *"High-Equity Same-Bye Build: Roster accepts a guaranteed scoring zero in Week X via a shared [QB/TE] bye. Premium stack correlation and player quality are strong enough to absorb the regular season bump to maximize your December tournament ceiling."*

### 4. Late-Season Rookie Upside Multiplier
- **Trigger**: The roster contains 5 or more rookies.
- **Evaluation**: In large-field best ball formats, late-season rookie progression is a core uniqueness and variance feature, not a structural floor risk.
- **Conditional Logic**: Do not issue a penalty for high rookie exposure. Instead, if a roster hits 5+ rookies while maintaining clean primary team stacks, trigger a **Strength Flag**: *"Asymmetric Rookie Ceiling: Roster heavily leverages 5+ rookies. Maximizes late-season depth chart acceleration and lineup uniqueness exactly when the tournament playoff window opens."*

## SECTION 6 — GAME THEORY & FIELD-SIZE CALIBRATION

### Field Size Overlays
**Always confirm tournament structure before any roster grade or draft advice. Never assume.**

- **Massive field (100k+ entries — BBM VII, Puppy):** Contrarian stacks and uniqueness premium. Roster uniqueness and geometric correlation score higher than nominal ADP value extraction.
- **Mid-field (10k–100k entries):** Balance correlation with individual upside. ADP value matters more than in massive fields.
- **Small expert field (under 5k entries — Main tournament):** Individual upside over correlation. Floor matters slightly more. Best player available over stack fit. Grade on standalone player quality and matchup strength across all three weeks equally.

### ADP Discipline
Treat market ADP as a soft variable, not a hard constraint. In large-field tournaments, uniqueness and correlation geometry outperform closing line value chasing. Avoid reaches driven by narrative accumulating over data. When a thesis requires multiple unconfirmed assumptions to be simultaneously true, that is the signal to step back — not a specific ADP number.

## SECTION 7B — ROSTER GRADING CRITERIA

### What Gets Graded — Primary Axes (in order of weight)

**1. Stack Loop Integrity**

Evaluate loop quality by depth chart reality and capital deployment, not piece count.

**Stack Classification — Full Loop:**
- QB + confirmed alpha on a one-alpha or thin offense = full credit
- QB + 2+ correlated pieces from an offense with real stackable depth = full credit, recognized as stronger architecture
- QB + 1 receiver when the offense clearly supported a stackable WR2 or TE at reachable ADP and that was skipped for unrelated players = soft informational flag only
- QB with zero correlated pieces = unlooped QB, hard penalty
- Receivers from a team with no QB on the roster = evaluate as partial stack, not automatic orphan

**Stack Classification — Partial Stack (No QB):**

2+ skill players from the same team without a QB on the roster. Partial stacks are not penalized as orphans. Grade on three factors:

1. **Game environment quality:** Does the team have a functional window across W15, W16, and W17? Apply full five-lens evaluation to the team's playoff schedule. Apply FPA Direction Rule — grade the matchups the team's offense faces, not the FPA rating of the team's own defense.
2. **Game-script correlation strength:** Two WRs from a pass-heavy offense share stronger correlation than a WR + RB from a run-heavy team. Score accordingly.
3. **Role confirmation on both players:** Two confirmed role players earn more credit than two darts with unconfirmed depth charts.

Partial stacks earn reduced credit relative to full loops but are not penalized. They represent intentional construction — diversity and bring-back capital prioritized over QB spend on that team.

**Stack Classification — Orphaned Single:**

One player from a team with no teammates on the roster. Not automatically a weak orphan. Apply the standard evaluation: functional three-week window + ADP edge check. Penalize only if no functional window exists AND no ADP edge AND no stack value anywhere on the roster.

---

**2. Three-Week Coverage**

Does the roster have live correlated pieces with functional playoff windows in W15, W16, AND W17? Coverage is measured relative to the roster's actual stacks — not against any universal preferred environment list. A dead week is a dead week regardless of which game caused it.

---

**3. Bring-Back Correlation Quality**

Graded relative to the QB's actual games only. A bring-back only earns credit if it ties to a game one of the roster's QBs is playing in. Evaluate whether that specific game has functional ceiling using the full five-lens framework — competitive balance, defensive quality, pace, blowout risk. No O/U gates.

A bring-back from a blowout-risk game (dominant team vs weak team, spread 7+, total below 44, clock-kill offense) earns reduced credit. A bring-back from a competitive two-good-offense game earns full credit even if raw defensive EPA looks average.

---

**4. Positional Construction Benchmarks**

BBM benchmarks as context, not constraint. Grade relative to what the capital bought. Flag meaningful deviations only when the construction created a structural problem — not simply because it diverged from the benchmark numbers.

---

**5. Orphan Count and Quality**

Weak orphan = no functional window in any of the three weeks AND no stack value AND no ADP edge. Apply the competitive balance check, blowout risk check, and FPA Direction Rule before classifying any player as an orphan. A player in a competitive game between two good offenses is not an orphan just because the raw total looks average. A player in a blowout-risk game on the trailing team gets flagged even if the defensive matchup looks soft.

Strong orphan = functional three-week window, just isolated from any stack. Flag as informational only, no penalty.

---

### What Does NOT Grade the Roster

Specific environment coverage (any named game or team cluster) is not a grading criterion. A roster intentionally built around different environments is not penalized for that choice. Grade the quality of the construction chosen, not whether it matched projected optimal environments.

O/U values are contextual inputs only. They inform competitive balance assessment and blowout risk checks. They do not gate grades.

### Grading Output Structure (required — do not alter headings)

**Playoff Schedule Matrix and Stack Analysis**

**Primary Stack Engine**
Identify the QB, all correlated pass catchers on the same team, loop depth, and internal structure. State whether it's a full loop (QB + confirmed alpha) or partial. Grade the QB's playoff schedule using FPA data for each week — apply FPA Direction Rule, competitive balance check, and blowout risk check separately.

**Secondary Stack Engine**
Same evaluation as primary. If no secondary QB stack exists, identify the strongest partial stack and evaluate it here instead.

**Partial Stacks (No QB)**
Identify every same-team cluster of 2+ players without a QB on the roster. For each partial stack grade:
- Game environment quality across W15/W16/W17 using FPA data (apply FPA Direction Rule — grade the defenses the team's offense faces, not the team's own defensive FPA)
- Game-script correlation strength (WR + WR from pass-heavy offense scores higher than WR + RB from run-heavy team)
- Role confirmation on both players

**Playoff Correlation Matrix**

Map every week explicitly. For each week identify:

1. Primary QB stack — which game, which players, which side
2. Secondary QB stack — which game, which players, which side
3. Bring-backs — for EVERY QB stack and partial stack, identify any players on the opposing side of that same game. State which QB or stack anchor the bring-back is tied to, the ADP cost, and whether it earns full/reduced/no credit
4. Partial stacks — which game, which side, window grade for that week
5. Game locks — flag explicitly when both sides of the same game are covered on the roster
6. Orphaned singles — players with no stack partner, evaluate window quality

Use this format per week:

**W[X] — [Games covered]**
- [Primary stack]: [players] + [team] — [side of game] — [FPA grade for that week]
- [Primary bring-back]: [players] + [team] — opposing side of [QB]'s W[X] game — [ADP] — [credit level]

- [Partial bring-back]: [players] + [team] — opposing side of partial stack's W[X] game — [ADP] — [credit level]
- [Game lock]: [players] — both sides of [game] confirmed
- [Orphan]: [player] + [team] — [window grade] — [ADP edge check]

Note dead weeks explicitly. A week with no live correlated pieces from any stack is a dead week regardless of which game caused it.

**Late-Round ADP Flattening Protocol (Picks 160+)**

Trigger: A player is drafted at pick 160 or later.

Evaluation: Market efficiency degrades significantly in the final rounds. Pure correlation, depth chart contingency, and W17 bring-back utility supersede nominal ADP value.

Conditional Logic:
- Do not trigger any "reach" or ADP-based deductions for players selected at pick 160+.
- Evaluate the pick exclusively on geometric fit (does it complete a W15-W17 stack loop?) or structural necessity (does it insulate a naked RB?).
- Flag severe reaches (40+ picks) as informational only. Do not apply a scored penalty.
