B.Words you will see in this document

Every term used anywhere below, in plain English. Nothing here assumes you follow the NFL. Read it once and the rest of the document will make sense.

ADPAverage draft position. Where a player usually gets picked. ADP 12.4 means he normally goes around the 12th pick overall. If he is still available well past that number, he is a bargain. It is a price, not a ranking.
PPRPoints per reception. This league is full PPR, so every catch is worth one point on top of the yards. A player who catches 100 passes banks 100 points before anything else happens. Most rankings online assume half a point, which is why they are quietly wrong for this league.
FlexA starting spot that accepts a running back, a receiver or a tight end. You have one. It means your seventh best skill player still scores for you.
BenchPlayers on your roster who are not in your starting lineup this week. You have six spots. They exist so you can swap somebody in when a starter is hurt or has a bad matchup.
Depth chartThe team's own published list of who is first, second and third at each position. First on the depth chart means he plays; third usually means he does not. Teams update these through preseason.
WorkhorseA running back who gets nearly all of his team's carries. The most valuable thing a running back can be, because volume is what produces points.
CommitteeThe opposite. Two or three backs splitting the work, so none of them gets enough to be reliable. When this document says "avoid, he is in a committee," that is the reason.
HandcuffThe backup to a running back you already own, drafted only in case yours gets hurt. Do not draft any in this league. With six bench spots you cannot afford a player who only has value if something goes wrong.
Waiver wireThe pool of players nobody has drafted, who you can add during the season. In a 16 team league this pool is basically empty, because 240 players are already taken. That is why the draft matters so much more here.
TargetsPasses thrown at a player, caught or not. Target share is the percentage of his team's throws that go to him. A 30% target share is elite. It matters more than catches, because it shows how much the team wants to use him.
Red zone
Goal line		The last 20 yards, and the last few yards, before the end zone. The player who gets the ball there scores the touchdowns. A back who does nothing else but take goal line carries can still be worth drafting.
Snaps
First-team reps		A snap is one play. First-team reps means practicing with the starters. Both are evidence of what a coach actually plans to do, which is more reliable than what a coach says.
Passing downA play where a pass is likely, usually third down. Some running backs only play on these, which caps their value in most leagues but not in this one, because catches are points here.
VacatedWork left behind when a player leaves or gets hurt. Those catches and carries go to somebody, and finding out who before the price moves is where most of the value in this document comes from.
Usable game
Bad game		Shorthand used throughout. A usable game means he scored enough to be worth starting. A bad game means he did not. "Only one bad game in 16" is a compliment about reliability, which matters because you set your lineup before the games, not after.
RookieA player in his first NFL season. There are no statistics for him at all, so every case rests on where he was drafted, what he is being paid, and what coaches have done with him in practice.
Injured reserve
PUP list		Two lists teams put injured players on. Being on either means he is not available right now. Coming off one is good news.
Playoff slateThe three specific games a player's team plays in weeks 15, 16 and 17, which is when your league is decided. "A soft slate" means those three opponents were bad at stopping his position last year. Section E covers this.

The risk: the honest reason he might not work out. Every sleeper has one of these. A list of exciting names with no downsides would read as though they are all going to be good, and they are not.

PPR bonussteadyRookie

The coloured box on the left is his position. The tags at the bottom mean:

  • PPR bonus he catches a lot of passes, so he gains more than most from this league's scoring. He is worth more here than his price says.
  • steady he gave a usable score nearly every week last season. Valuable because you choose your lineup in advance.
  • Rookie first NFL season, no statistics exist yet.

A red Avoid box at the end of a round names the players who look tempting at that price and should be skipped, with the reason. Reading those is as valuable as reading the recommendations.
