D.Sleepers, by position

Players going in round 5 or later whose role is getting bigger. These are the names most people have not heard of, which is exactly why they are cheap. Every one has a specific stated reason, and every one also has the honest risk written next to it.

Rookie means he has never played an NFL regular season game, so there are no statistics to look at. The case rests entirely on draft position, contract, and what coaches have actually done with him in practice. PPR bonus means he gains extra from the one point per catch scoring.

Running backs

RB
Chris RodriguezJAX · Round 10ADP 157.8

Named a co-starter on Jacksonville's first depth chart on Aug 15, which is a real climb because he opened camp on the injury list after foot surgery. The shape of the role is what matters more than the label: he is the short yardage and goal line back while the other back handles passing downs. That means he holds the touchdown work in one of the best running offenses in the league.
The risk: his value is touchdown dependent rather than volume dependent, so his weeks will swing. The foot surgery is also recent.

RB
Tyler AllgeierARI · Round 10ADP 159.1

Listed as the number one back on Arizona's first depth chart, ahead of a rookie taken third overall. He signed a real contract, not a minimum deal, which is how teams signal intent. The number behind him: 19 carries inside the opponent's 20 yard line on only 16 targets, the most concentrated short yardage workload in the data.
Updated Aug 26, and this is now the biggest upgrade on the page: the rookie who was supposed to take his job, Jeremiyah Love, has a high ankle sprain, will not play again in preseason, and is hoping to be ready for Week 1. Arizona also released Trey Benson, so the depth behind Allgeier is a 31 year old James Conner and not much else. The job is his to start the season and the price has not moved.
The risk: Love is still the third overall pick and takes the job back when healthy. This is a strong start, not a season.

RB
Braelon AllenNYJ · Round 9ADP 141.0

The starter ahead of him strained his groin on Aug 17 and is out two to three weeks, and Allen is the outright next man up rather than one of two options. His only competition is a returner and two undrafted rookies. He is also back from a knee injury that cost him most of last season and is reported bigger and stronger.
Updated Aug 26: he has backed it up on the field. A 31 yard touchdown run in the first preseason game, two more touchdowns after that, and he outgained the starter on the same number of carries in the second game.
The risk: the starter is expected back for week 1, so this is insurance rather than a job. It pays off if that groin lingers or recurs.

RB
Jadarian PriceSEA · Round 5ADP 66.5Rookie

First round rookie, and the back who would have competed with him tore his ACL. He opened camp second on the depth chart, then took the first team rep in the first full scrimmage on Jul 30, and beat reporters now describe the job as increasingly his. His work catching passes has been specifically called out as a standout.
Updated Aug 26, and this one cuts against him: the published depth chart still lists him as the number two back behind George Holani. Camp reports and the depth chart disagree, which means the job is contested rather than won.
The risk: that contradiction, plus lower body soreness in early August. This is the earliest sleeper on the list and the least of a bargain.

RB
Kaytron AllenWAS · Round 11ADP 171.0Rookie

23 carries for 85 yards and a touchdown, plus a catch, in the preseason opener. That is an unusually heavy workload for a first exhibition and it means the staff wanted a long look at him. Washington runs the ball a lot and kept the same offensive scheme.
The risk: he averaged only 3.7 yards on those carries, so this is a volume signal rather than a production one, and the backfield is still crowded. A lottery ticket with a real reason behind it.

RB
Jonah ColemanDEN · Round 9ADP 131.2Rookie

Camp reports have him trending toward an every down role because the back ahead of him struggles in pass protection. Pass protection is the single most common reason a young back gets kept off the field, so a rookie who can do it takes snaps quickly.
The risk: camp reports are not a depth chart. Nothing here is confirmed.

RB
Kenneth GainwellTB · Round 8ADP 113.1

73 catches last year, and his new team confirmed in camp that catching passes is the actual job rather than a rotation. He lines up in the backfield and in the slot as the primary safety valve. His coordinator's backs at his previous team totalled 1,435 receiving yards over two seasons.
The risk: those 73 catches came with a different team, quarterback and coordinator, so do not assume they transfer whole. His carries are capped by the starter ahead of him.

PPR bonus

RB
Jonathon BrooksCAR · Round 8ADP 113.4

Added Aug 26. He has recovered from two knee surgeries and is running with the first team, and his head coach has said he and the incumbent will share the job to start the season. The incumbent is also currently injured. His price is climbing fast in drafts right now, so he may be gone earlier than round 8.
The risk: two knee surgeries, and it is a committee rather than a job.

RB
MarShawn LloydGB · Round 13+very late

Added Aug 26. Green Bay's starter has a groin injury on top of an existing off-field situation, and Lloyd has locked up the number two job after staying healthy through the whole preseason. He is going very late or undrafted.
The risk: injuries ended each of his first two seasons. That is the entire reason he is free.

Wide receivers

WR
Wandale RobinsonTEN · Round 6ADP 86.6

The best value on this entire page for this scoring format. 92 catches for 1,014 yards on a 29.8% share of his team's targets, and the market has not raised his price. The competition is thinner than the price implies: one is an unproven rookie and the other averaged 2.4 catches a game last year.
Updated Aug 26, and it got better: he led the team scrimmage with nine catches, his quarterback publicly confirmed him as the slot receiver, and his head coach from his old team is now his offensive coordinator. That is continuity most players changing teams do not get.
The risk: his quarterback has been poor this preseason, 54.2% completions with no touchdowns on 24 attempts. The role is confirmed, the offense around it is not.

PPR bonus

WR
Josh DownsIND · Round 6ADP 91.2

Indianapolis traded away the receiver who caught the short and middle passes, and Downs is built to catch exactly those. The other receiver on the team plays the outside, so there is no overlap between them. His role goes from part time slot to the main middle of field target.
Updated Aug 26: the number behind it is strong. He was targeted on 24% of his routes when he was on the field last year, so the only thing that was ever missing was snaps.
The risk: it depends on the quarterback staying healthy. That same quarterback got hurt last season and the offense collapsed.

WR
Denzel BostonCLE · Round 9ADP 131.4Rookie

Listed as Cleveland's number one receiver on the early depth chart, and his coordinator has said he moves into the first team offense. A rookie handed the top job outright is a completely different asset from a rookie competing for snaps, and his price has not caught up to that.
Updated Aug 26: he has held it. He played with the first unit in both two and three receiver sets in preseason and has been the talk of camp for his route running and hands. Cleveland also settled its quarterback job, naming Deshaun Watson the Week 1 starter on Aug 24, so the offense around him is at least defined now.
The risk: early depth charts are reversible, and Cleveland was a poor offense last year.

WR
Malik WashingtonMIA · Round 10ADP 153.2

Miami's de facto number one receiver in camp and his quarterback's most targeted option, taking the bulk of first team reps. He had been written off as a short pass specialist, and camp contradicted that directly with a 60 yard catch and a 45 yard catch and run in a single session.
The risk: the quarterback is unproven and the offense projects to run the ball. These are preseason reps, not confirmed roles.

WR
Tre HarrisLAC · Round 11ADP 167.5

The third receiver job is effectively his, because the veteran ahead of him left and the only competition is a fifth round pick and a rookie. Second round draft capital, and his head coach called him a matchup nightmare, which is role language rather than generic praise.
The risk: he is third in line for targets. His 2025 numbers were poor, though he was used mostly as a blocker under a coordinator who has since been fired.

WR
Germie BernardPIT · Round 13ADP 197.0Rookie

Second round rookie who led his offense through the first half of the preseason opener, working mainly from the slot. That matters because the two established receivers on his team both play outside, so he complements them rather than competing with them. He out-snapped his direct competitor in the slot by more than two to one, and injuries in that receiver room widened the opening.
The risk: this is a first exhibition game, which is evaluation rather than confirmation. Essentially free at this price.

WR
Jakobi LaneBAL · Round 14ADP 216.0Rookie

Third round rookie taking first team reps as the clear standout of Baltimore's camp, with red zone chemistry with his quarterback called out specifically, while a veteran ahead of him missed practice. His price moved about 22 spots in seven days on that news alone.
The risk: preseason reps before the third week are scheme evaluation, not a role. Cheap enough that it does not matter much if it fails.

WR
Elic AyomanorTEN · Round 15ADP 227.0

Second year receiver running neck and neck with a first round rookie for the best performer in camp, consistent on back shoulder throws and deep routes, and a big target over the middle for a young quarterback.
The risk: he is contested at the same spot by a player drafted 155 picks earlier, and the quarterback throwing to him has been poor this preseason, 54.2% completions with no touchdowns.

WR
Malik BensonLV · Round 13ADP 200.0Rookie

Reported as a top three receiver on his team through six practices with first team reps and chemistry with the number one draft pick. Pure deep threat, 6 foot 1 and 195 pounds running 4.37, and he caught 11 of 17 targets thrown more than 20 yards last year in college.
The risk: the weakest case on this list. Sixth round pick, four different colleges, about two catches a game in college, and the number three job is genuinely not won. A free roll, nothing more.

Quarterbacks

QB
Kyler MurrayMIN · Round 9ADP 142.7

Won the starting job on Aug 12, with the competition sent to the bench and no conditions attached. He took the larger share of first team reps through camp. He was usable in every game he played last year. Getting a confirmed week 1 starter in round 9 is the whole argument for waiting on quarterback.
The risk: he is back from a foot injury and his availability record, not his role, is the open question.

QB
Patrick MahomesKC · Round 6ADP 94.9

Cleared for full camp with no limitations about eight months after knee surgery, and his coach confirmed full strength returned. He is scrambling without protecting the leg. His short 2025 season was that December injury, not a change in his role.
Updated Aug 26: he has not missed a practice in camp, but his head coach has stopped short of confirming him for Week 1. Both of those are true at once.
The risk: a two ligament reconstruction in his plant leg. Check the morning of the draft.

QB
Caleb WilliamsCHI · Round 7ADP 97.2

Third year is typically when young quarterbacks make their jump, and his coordinator was kept, which means he is not learning a new offense for the third time in three years. That continuity is the actual case here.
The risk: the leap is expected rather than evidenced. This is a projection.

Tight ends

There is no real sleeper tier at tight end this year, and that is worth saying plainly rather than inventing one. The position splits into two useful groups and a gap in the middle.

TE
Harold FanninCLE · Round 5ADP 70.6

72 catches as a rookie on a 31% share of his team's targets. Not unknown exactly, but priced two rounds below where that production usually costs. This is the fallback if McBride and Bowers are gone.
